Working At CIBC

Introduction

Hi everyone! My name is Thanush, and I am pursuing a Bachelor of Computing in Computer Science (co-op) at the University of Guelph. As of now, I have completed around four years of my degree and am in the process of finishing my fourth co-op work term. My fourth co-op term was with CIBC (Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce) as a Software/Application Developer Co-op working on BPSA and WEB apps under Client Operations Technology. I was fortunate to return to the bank for my fourth co-op term although in a different position, which allowed me to explore different area's of the bank and what applications are being used.

At CIBC, I had the opportunity to work on an internal project that allowed me to gain various skills in development, testing, as well as application support.


Information About The Employer

CIBC is widely known as one of the top five banks in Canada. It serves over 14 million personal banking, business, public sector, and institutional clients in Canada, the United States, and around the world. The office where I worked is located at 81 Bay Street on the 14th floor of CIBC Square. The areas of computer science most relevant to my role at CIBC include Software Development, Application Support, Business Systems Analysis and Testing and Quality Assurance.


Interesting Facts

  • The company was founded in 1867 and has since grown to become one of the biggest and best banks in the country.
  • CIBC operates out of three main locations in downtown Toronto: its headquarters at 81 Bay Street, known as CIBC Square; 161 Bay Street, also known as the TD Canada Trust Tower, which includes the trade floor; and 595 Bay Street, referred to as Atrium on Bay.
  • There were over 200 winter interns this work term, divided amongst many sections within the bank.


Learning Goals

My 5 goals were...

  1. Gain a Better Understanding of the IRIS Web Codebase and Project.
  2. Gain a Better Understanding of the Business Perspective Behind the Project.
  3. Improve My Judgment on When to Ask for Help
  4. Develop a Better Understanding of the Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC) in a Real Project
  5. Continue Networking and Building Professional Relationships Across the Bank

By the end of the term, I wanted to achieve a solid understanding of my role and responsibilities as a Software/Application Developer Co-op. My goal was not only to learn the day-to-day tasks but also to gain insight into how my work contributes to larger team objectives and organizational success here at CIBC. By creating these 5 goals, I aimed to ensure that I would have a productive and rewarding co-op experience. Overall, setting these goals allowed me to grow as a professional, to step outside my comfort zone, and to prepare for future roles in technology. They helped me stay accountable and ensured that my co-op term was both meaningful and impactful.


Job Description And Projects

During this co-op term, my role involved making minor code changes based on business needs and resolving errors that arose within the application. I also worked on documentation and took the lead on performance testing for my team. These responsibilities gave me valuable hands-on experience with real-world software development, helping me build a stronger understanding of how technical work supports business operations. I was fortunate to work alongside an amazing team that provided me with unique experiences and learning opportunities, allowing me to further grow as a student.


Conclusion

This co-op term has been one of the most valuable experiences I've had so far in my academic journey. Coming in, I set five goals for myself: gaining a better understanding of the codebase, understanding the business perspective behind the project, improving my judgment on when to ask for help, developing a better understanding of the Software Development Lifecycle, and continuing to build professional relationships across the bank. Looking back, I'm really glad I took the time to set those goals because they gave me something concrete to work toward throughout the term. The first few weeks were honestly a bit of an adjustment. There was a lot to take in, a new codebase, new processes, and a new team. I spent a lot of time reading through code, reviewing documentation, and asking questions. Over time though, things started to click and I began taking on more meaningful work, including making minor code changes based on business needs, fixing errors that came up in the application, contributing to documentation, and leading performance testing for my team. That last one was especially rewarding because it pushed me to take ownership of something and see it through. One of the bigger takeaways from this term was getting to see the Software Development Lifecycle play out in a real environment. It's one thing to learn about it in school, but actually being part of the process, watching tickets move from planning to development to testing, gave me a much better sense of how things work in practice. I also got better at knowing when to push through a problem on my own and when it made more sense to ask for help, which is something I struggled with early on. Outside of the technical side, I made an effort to connect with people across the organization and I'm really glad I did. The conversations I had gave me a lot of perspective on different roles and career paths, and the relationships I built with my team made the whole experience that much better. I'm walking away from this term with stronger technical skills, a better understanding of how business and technology connect, and a lot more confidence heading into future roles.
